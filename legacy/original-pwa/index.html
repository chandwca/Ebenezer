Why it works this way

The path, from Jesus’ own encounters

At the well, Jesus met a woman in her thirst and her story before he offered living water (John 4). On the hillside, he fed five thousand before he taught them (John 6). In Peter’s boat, he filled empty nets, then said, “Fear not; from henceforth thou shalt catch men” (Luke 5). Meet the need, talk, then call to follow. Ebenezer’s path does the same: how do you feel → find a Word → read the chapter → reflect → pray and share with someone → set a stone.

What reviewers and interviewees told us

  • Orwell: start from common emotions like overwhelmed and anxious, lead to a searchable list of verses, open the chapter, then ask what you learned, what questions you have, your thoughts, and how we can pray. Find someone to pray with and share with, then set a stone. Make the look more appealing, and keep things people can archive: a book, songs, their story, a message board.
  • Jonathan: generic emotions alone didn’t help him, so the first step also offers “What happened” (parents, work, two homes) and a search box.
  • Mr. Mlanga: verses alone won’t keep faith, so every stone is a conversation with a passage and a person.
  • Brittany: it’s about the soil.
  • Aaron: do one thing really well: set a stone, and find it again.

The rule

No Scripture, no stone. Every stone is built from a passage, your reflection on it, and, if you choose, the people you asked to pray.

Prototype. The calendar, groups and prayer board are sample data. Scripture is KJV (public domain), copied from source text; Bible stories are retold in our own words. “Read the whole chapter” opens the KJV on bible.com.
